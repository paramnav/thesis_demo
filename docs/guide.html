1. Extrapolation: what each model must do

Inside the training support (interpolation)

All three classes can approximate the coastal–shelf gradient because labels and features co-occur. Errors here are mostly bias–variance and noise, not “no data.”

Outside the training support (extrapolation / covariate shift)

Behaviour is dictated by the function class, not by held-out $R^2$ on random splits:

Model Mathematical constraint Typical offshore behaviour
Random forest Leaf prediction = mean of training $y$ in that leaf → bounded by convex hull of labels Plateau or patchy constant from shelf-dominated leaves
$k$-NN Average of $k$ nearest training labels → also in convex hull Value of nearest shelf cores in feature space (geography not explicit)
MLP (ReLU) Piecewise-linear map; not limited to $[\min y_i, \max y_i]$ Can continue slopes beyond training; continuation may be wrong

The caption for Figure extrapolation_failure states this correctly: RF flattens, $k$-NN “sticks” to neighbours, MLP can trend up or down offshore.

Random forest (max_depth, n_estimators)

  • For $x>6$, predictions are exactly constant along the line (numerical std $=0$ in $x\in[6,10]$).
  • Value is ~7.9–8.0: essentially the high-plateau leaf mean from cores near $x\approx 6$.
  • More trees (5 → 300): slightly smoother inside training range; extrapolation still flat.
  • Depth (2 → 25): more wiggles in $[0,6]$; offshore still a single level — cannot predict below ~7.9 or above training max (~8.7) in this setup.

Implication: RF “extrapolation” is not extrapolation in a physical sense; it is label-space pooling from the nearest dense region.

$k$-NN (hyperparameter $k$)

$k$ $x=9$ prediction Mechanism
1 ~7.86 Nearest point at $x\approx 6$ (high plateau)
5 ~7.97 Average of a few high-$x$ neighbours
10–40 ~8.0 Smoother, closer to local high mean
80 ~6.44 Many neighbours include low plateau points → pulled toward global mean
  • Small $k$: high variance, stair-steps, locks to boundary cores (matches figure caption).
  • Large $k$: bias toward global mean (~6.4 here) — can undershoot the true 8.0 offshore.
  • Still cannot leave $[\min y_i, \max y_i]$ (~[2.25, 8.74] for this draw).

MLP (architecture & regularization)

Config $x=9$ vs true 8.0 Comment
64–16, BN + dropout (thesis-like) +0.70 Smooth upward continuation past training max
64–32, BN + dropout +1.18 Stronger overshoot
64–32, no BN/dropout −0.83 More conservative; can undershoot offshore
8-unit shallow ReLU +9.25 Unstable extrapolation — avoid tiny nets for global maps
  • BN + dropout: better interpolation, but extrapolation can exceed any observed TOC (violates convex-hull safety).
  • Depth/width: deeper/wider → stronger linear extrapolation of the last active ReLU pieces.
  • Risk: low in-sample error + confident wrong trend offshore → needs MC dropout / information gain.

3. Figure patchiness (2D shelf transect)

True TOC offshore at 78 km is ~0.9–1.9 wt.%; shelf cores are ~4–9 wt.%.

Shelf-only training (top row)

Probe True RF $k$-NN MLP
Coast (interp.) 8.70 8.67 8.77 8.51
Shelf mid 5.25 5.03 5.00 4.97
Offshore shallow 1.91 4.77 4.46 2.83
Offshore deep 0.88 4.28 4.04 2.93
  • RF & $k$-NN: offshore maps are much too high (~4 wt.% means) — they paint the yellow zone with shelf-like values.
  • MLP: closer to truth (~2.8) but still ~2× too high; smooth shelf–basin transition, not axis-aligned blocks.
  • Offshore strip at $x=72$ km: true mean 1.59 vs RF 4.39 (low variance, ~plateau) vs MLP 2.78 (some depth structure).

Visual reading (fig_patchiness_one_point.pdf):

  • RF: rectangular patches aligned with coast/depth splits.
  • $k$-NN: radial streaks from Euclidean distance in scaled $(\text{coast}, \text{depth})$.
  • MLP: smooth field, modest offshore gradient.

After one offshore core (bottom row; star at 78 km, 3600 m)

Probe True RF $k$-NN MLP
Offshore deep (at core) 0.88 3.24 3.09 0.79
Far offshore 0.50 3.24 3.09 0.79
  • One label offshore pulls RF/$k$-NN down only partially (still ~3 wt.% at the core; plateau elsewhere).
  • MLP can move sharply toward the new core (~0.79 at depth) — most responsive to new data, but still wrong far from the core without more samples.
  • Offshore strip std: RF 0.17 → 0.48; MLP 0.26 → 1.07 (heterogeneous surface after one point).

Data lesson: a single extrapolation core does not fix global offshore mapping; it locally retunes the MLP and slightly shifts tree/instance averages.

5. Synthesis: how models differ under extrapolation

flowchart LR
  subgraph train ["Training domain"]
    A["Shelf cores dense"]
  end
  subgraph extrap ["Extrapolation zone"]
    B["No or few cores"]
  end
  A --> RF["RF: leaf mean of shelf labels"]
  A --> KNN["k-NN: avg nearest shelf labels"]
  A --> MLP["MLP: continue ReLU linear pieces"]
  RF --> R1["Plateau / patches too high"]
  KNN --> R2["Streaks / neighbour lock-in"]
  MLP --> R3["Smooth trend may overshoot or undershoot"]

Ranking for global ocean mapping (extrapolation-heavy setting)

  1. Interpolation near cores: all can work; trees often strong on tabular benchmarks.
  2. Offshore with no cores: none is trustworthy without uncertainty; RF/$k$-NN are systematically biased high in the 2D shelf demo; MLP is closer but still wrong without UQ.
  3. Adding sparse offshore cores: MLP adapts most smoothly; RF/$k$-NN update locally/patchily.
  4. Allowing physically new TOC values: only MLP (and TabPFN, with caveats) can predict outside observed range — benefit and liability.

When hyperparameters matter most for extrapolation

Hyperparameter Effect on extrapolation
RF depth Coarser leaves → stronger constant offshore plateaus
$k$ Small $k$ → boundary locking; very large $k$ → global mean collapse
MLP width/depth/regularization Controls slope of offshore continuation; shallow/unregularized nets are dangerous

Bottom line

Under extrapolation, the three classes do not merely differ in accuracy — they implement different definitions of “prediction”:

  • RF and $k$-NN re-export shelf training statistics into feature-space deserts.
  • MLP extends learned trends that may leave the observed label range.

Hyperparameters tune how flat, how spiky, or how steep that failure mode is; data placement (offshore cores, outliers) determines where the map can change at all.

For the thesis, any claim of offshore skill should be paired with spatial holdout, covariate-shift awareness, and uncertainty — especially for the MLP, where extrapolation can look plausible while being physically uncoupled from the true process.
